The Nelder-Mead Algorithm

Also known as the Downhill Simplex Method or the "Amoeba" Method.

What is it?

Nelder-Mead is a numerical method to find the minimum (or maximum) of a function. Unlike "Gradient Descent" used in Deep Learning, Nelder-Mead does not use derivatives (calculus).

This makes it perfect for your Quantum Physics simulations where:

  • The function is a "Black Box" (e.g., a Strawberry Fields simulation).
  • Calculating the mathematical slope (gradient) is impossible or computationally expensive.
  • The landscape might be slightly "noisy" due to numerical precision.

The Geometric Intuition

Imagine you are on a foggy mountain and want to find the lowest valley. You can't see the slope, but you have a GPS altimeter.

1. The Simplex

The algorithm drops a geometric shape onto the landscape. In 2D (optimizing Alpha and r), this shape is a Triangle (3 points).

2. The Logic

It evaluates the "Cost" (1 - Fidelity) at the 3 corners. It identifies the Worst corner (highest cost) and tries to flip it away to find a better spot.

3. The Movement

The triangle behaves like a living amoeba. It stretches to go downhill fast, shrinks to fit into narrow valleys, and flips over itself.

The 4 Moves

At every step, the algorithm sorts the vertices: Best (B), Good (G), and Worst (W). It then tries to replace W.

🪞 1. Reflection

Flip the Worst point over the line between Best and Good.
"Let's move away from the bad area."

🚀 2. Expansion

If Reflection was amazing (better than Best), go further in that direction.
"We found a steep downhill slope, accelerate!"

🤏 3. Contraction

If Reflection wasn't great, don't go that far. Try a point halfway.
"Careful, let's test the water first."

📉 4. Shrink

If everything fails, the valley is very narrow. Shrink the whole triangle towards the Best point.
"Huddle together."
